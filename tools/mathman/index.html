How sin, cos, tan, and their inverses read plain numbers. A unit you type always wins: sin(30 deg) is 0.5 either way. The DEG / RAD button above the worksheet switches it too.

Help & Shortcuts

Layout

Library (left)
Your sheets, the variables and functions the current sheet defines, a reference of every function, constant, and unit, and the results you saved to History.
Workbench (center)
The worksheet: type one calculation per line and each answer appears beside it. The Result card shows the line the caret is on in full, with its exact fraction and a copy button.
Tools (right)
A keypad, a unit converter, a graph, statistics, an equation solver, number theory, percentages, and finance. Most tools can send their answer to the worksheet as a new line.

Click a pane's title to open it; one pane per side is open at a time. Drag a title to reorder the panes. On a phone, the two buttons at the top right slide each side in.

Operators, from loosest to tightest

OperatorMeaningExample
to · as · inConvert the result (last of all)100 km/h to mph

+ -Add and subtract5 km + 300 m
* / modMultiply, divide, remainder; implicit multiplication sits here too17 mod 5
-xNegative-2^2 = -4
^Power, grouped from the right2^3^2 = 512
! %Factorial and percent, right after a number5! · 15%

Operators of equal rank work left to right, so 1/2x is (1/2) × x and 8 / 2 / 2 is 2. Use parentheses whenever you want a different order.

After /, a number and the unit right after it are one quantity: 5 km / 2 h is 2.5 km/h and 60 / 1 min is 60 per minute. The one exception is a plain fraction typed without spaces in front of a unit, like 1/2 cup or pi/2 rad: that is that fraction of the unit. % means percent, never remainder; for a remainder use 10 mod 3.

Implicit multiplication

Leave out the * when the meaning is clear: 2x, 2(3 + 4), (1 + 2)(3 + 4), 2pi, 3 sqrt(4), and x y all multiply. A function name followed by ( is a call; a variable followed by ( multiplies. Turn this off in Settings to require * every time.

Units and conversion

Write a unit after a number: 5 km, 3 feet, 2.5 kWh. Units mix when they measure the same thing (5 km + 300 m is 5.3 km), build up from expressions (3 ft * 2 ft is an area, 100 kg * 9.8 m/s^2 to N is a force), and convert with to, as, or in. On its own, in means inches; after a complete quantity and before a unit, as in 100 km in miles, it converts.

Long names work with or without a hyphen (2 kilowatt-hours, 2 kilowatt hours), and per, squared, and cubed join units: 9.8 meters per second squared is 9.8 m/s². Products and quotients become a named unit when one fits exactly: 60 W * 2 h is 120 Wh and 10 V / 2 ohm is 5 A.

Covered: length, area, volume, mass, time, speed, acceleration, force, pressure, energy, power, temperature, data, angle, frequency, and electric units, with SI prefixes, long names, and plurals. Temperatures convert and add to the same unit, but °C × °C is refused, and nothing can be colder than absolute zero (−273.15 °C). The Reference pane lists every unit.

Variables and functions

price = 80
Defines a variable; later lines can use price. Names use letters, digits, and _, and can't reuse a function, constant, or unit name.
f(x) = x^2 + 1
Defines a function; f(3) on a later line gives 10. Functions can take several inputs: g(x, y) = x y.
Built in
sqrt, cbrt, root, abs, round, floor, ceil, min, max, sum, avg, median, stdev, gcd, lcm, nCr, ln, log, sin, cos, tan, if, and more — the Reference pane lists them all. Constants: pi (π), tau, e, phi.

The value of the nearest line above that has one.

The value on line 3, counting from 1.

Numbers, bases, and conversion targets

Write 42, 3.14, .5, 1e-3, 1_000_000, or other bases: 0xFF (hex), 0b1010 (binary), 0o17 (octal). End a line with to hex, to bin, to oct, to dec, to sci, to fraction, to %, or to a unit to change how its answer is shown (in hex works too).

Commas group thousands only outside brackets: 1,000 * 2 is 2,000. Inside brackets a comma separates values, so write max(1000, 2) or max(1_000, 2); mathMan points out a 1,000 it finds there.

Adding, subtracting, multiplying, dividing, and whole-number powers stay exact, so 1/3 + 1/6 is exactly 1/2 and 0.1 + 0.2 is exactly 0.3. Square roots, logarithms, and trigonometry use ordinary floating point.

Limits

A sheet holds up to 2,000 lines of up to 2,000 characters each (200,000 characters in all), and you can keep 200 sheets. Exact whole numbers stay exact up to about 6,000 digits and factorials up to 1000!; beyond that mathMan switches to floating point, and a result too large even for that, such as 9^9^9, shows an error instead of freezing the page. Functions can call themselves up to 64 levels deep.
